# Curation guidelines — Enacted AI Laws

This section lists AI laws that are actually in force. It is hand-curated — a person
reviews and approves every entry. These rules decide what goes in.

## A law is included only if ALL of these are true

1. **Enacted and in force.** It has completed every required legislative step and
   is (or will on a known date be) enforceable law. Nothing pending, nothing
   unenforceable.
2. **Applies to AI.** It may be AI-specific, or a general privacy or technology
   law with provisions that apply to AI systems.
3. **Any jurisdiction.** Countries, the EU, supranational bodies, US states —
   what qualifies a law is enactment, not geography.
4. **Enforceable.** Statutes qualify by default. Executive orders, agency rules,
   and guidelines qualify only if they carry enforcement power (binding
   obligations, penalties). Non-binding guidance, voluntary codes, and strategy
   documents do not.
5. **Verified against official government sources.** The law's text, status, and
   dates are cross-checked with the issuing government's official publication —
   not secondary reporting alone.

## Excluded

- Bills, drafts, and proposals — no matter how far along.
- Laws passed by only one chamber of a bicameral legislature.
- Unenforceable instruments: non-binding guidance, voluntary codes, white
  papers, national strategies.
- Repealed or fully superseded laws (remove or annotate on discovery).
- Anything that cannot be verified against an official government source.

## Each entry must have

- Official name + legal citation (e.g. "Regulation (EU) 2024/1689")
- Jurisdiction
- Date enacted and date in force
- Link to the legal text (official government source)
- A 1–2 sentence neutral summary — no advocacy language, no speculation about impact

## Maintenance

- Review the list quarterly. Laws get amended, repealed, or newly passed.
- Keep a written record of deliberate exclusions and the reason, so a future
  curator doesn't re-add them. Example: Brazil's PL 2338/2023 passed the Senate
  in Dec 2024 but remains pending in the Chamber of Deputies — not law, not listed.
