# Curation guidelines — Enacted AI Laws Worldwide

This section lists AI laws that are actually in force. It is hand-curated — a person
reviews and approves every entry. These rules decide what goes in.

## A law is included only if ALL of these are true

1. **Enacted, not proposed.** It has completed every required legislative step —
   passed all chambers *and* signed/promulgated (or the local equivalent).
   A bill that passed one chamber, is awaiting signature, or is still in
   committee does not qualify. (Example of a correct exclusion: Brazil's
   PL 2338/2023 passed the Senate in Dec 2024 but remains pending in the
   Chamber of Deputies — not law, not listed.)
2. **AI-specific.** Artificial intelligence is the law's primary subject, not an
   incidental mention inside a general privacy, cybersecurity, or consumer
   protection statute.
3. **Any jurisdiction.** Countries, the EU, supranational bodies, *and* US states.
   What qualifies a law is that it is enacted and in force — not where it was
   passed. (This is why Texas's TRAIGA and Connecticut's CART Act are listed
   here alongside the EU AI Act.) Pending bills, wherever they're from, belong
   in the US Federal/State Bills sections instead.
4. **Verifiable.** The entry links to the official legal text (preferred) or an
   authoritative government source. A reputable secondary source is acceptable
   only as a stopgap and should be replaced when a primary source is found.
5. **Dated.** Both the enactment date and the in-force date (or phased
   effective dates) are known and stated.

## Excluded

- Bills, drafts, proposals, and white papers — no matter how far along.
- Laws passed by only one chamber of a bicameral legislature.
- Executive orders, agency guidance, voluntary codes, and national AI
  strategies — these are not enacted laws.
- General data/tech laws with incidental AI provisions.
- Repealed or fully superseded laws (remove or annotate on discovery).
- Anything whose enactment cannot be verified from a primary or authoritative
  source.

## Each entry must have

- Official name + legal citation (e.g. "Regulation (EU) 2024/1689")
- Jurisdiction
- Date enacted and date in force
- Link (official legal text preferred)
- A 1–2 sentence neutral summary of what the law does — no advocacy language,
  no speculation about impact

## Maintenance

- Review the list quarterly. Laws get amended, repealed, or newly passed.
- Keep a written record of deliberate exclusions and the reason, so a future
  curator doesn't re-add them.
- When a pending bill you're watching becomes law, move it here and delete it
  from any watchlist — don't list it in both places.
