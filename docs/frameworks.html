AI Governance Frameworks

The soft-law layer: voluntary frameworks, codes, and principles that companies use to demonstrate responsible AI — and to get ahead of binding law.

The index · 14

Frameworks worth knowing

None of these are binding law on their own — that is the point. They are the instruments companies voluntarily adopt to manage AI risk, and several create a presumption of compliance with binding regimes.

Coming next: cross-walks mapping each framework to the binding laws it helps satisfy.

Risk management framework

NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)

US National Institute of Standards and Technology · United States · 2023

Voluntary framework for managing AI risks across the system lifecycle, organized around four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. The most widely referenced starting point for corporate AI governance programs.

Risk management framework

NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1)

US National Institute of Standards and Technology · United States · 2024

Companion profile applying the AI RMF to generative AI: catalogs gen-AI-specific risks (confabulation, dangerous content, IP, environmental) and maps suggested mitigations to the core framework functions.

Read the official text ↗
Management system standard

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — AI Management Systems

ISO / IEC · International · 2023

Certifiable management-system standard for AI: requires policies, risk assessment, and lifecycle controls for developing or using AI. The standard companies get independently audited against.

Read the official text ↗
Principles

OECD AI Principles

Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development · International · 2019 (revised 2024)

The first intergovernmental principles on AI: inclusive growth, human rights, transparency, robustness, and accountability. Adopted by OECD members and adherents; revised in 2024.

Read the official text ↗
Code of conduct

G7 Hiroshima Process — International Code of Conduct for Organizations Developing Advanced AI Systems

G7 · International · 2023

Voluntary code for developers of advanced AI systems: risk assessment across the lifecycle, incident reporting, transparency, and security practices. The G7's soft-law complement to binding regulation.

Read the official text ↗
Declaration

Bletchley Declaration

UK AI Safety Summit · United Kingdom / International · 2023

Declaration signed by 28 countries at the 2023 UK AI Safety Summit affirming international cooperation on frontier AI risks. A political commitment, not a binding instrument.

Read the official text ↗
Safety commitments

Frontier AI Safety Commitments (AI Seoul Summit)

16 AI companies via the AI Seoul Summit · International · 2024

Sixteen frontier AI companies committed to publish safety frameworks, assess severe risks, and pause development where mitigations are inadequate. The industry's most concrete voluntary safety pledge to date.

Read the official text ↗
Code of practice

EU General-Purpose AI Code of Practice

European Commission / EU AI Office · European Union · 2025

Voluntary code helping general-purpose AI model providers demonstrate compliance with the EU AI Act's duties on transparency, copyright, and systemic-risk evaluation. Signing creates a presumption of conformity.

Read the official text ↗
Principles

UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of AI

UNESCO · International · 2021

First global standard-setting instrument on AI ethics, adopted by 193 member states: human rights, fairness, transparency, and accountability as the values AI should serve.

Read the official text ↗
Governance framework

Model AI Governance Framework for Generative AI

Singapore IMDA / AI Verify Foundation · Singapore · 2024

Practical guidance for deploying generative AI responsibly: incident reporting, pre-deployment testing, and content provenance. Pairs with AI Verify, Singapore's open-source model-testing toolkit.

Read the official text ↗
Industry commitments

White House Voluntary AI Commitments

The White House · United States · 2023

Fifteen leading AI companies committed to pre-release safety testing, information sharing, watermarking AI-generated content, and public reporting of capabilities and limitations. The US flagship voluntary approach of 2023.

Read the official text ↗
Guidelines

AI Guidelines for Business (Ver. 1.0)

Japan METI + Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications · Japan · 2024

Non-binding guidance for AI developers, providers, and business users: risk-based governance, safety, transparency, and fairness duties. Unified three prior guidelines and aligns with the Hiroshima Process.

Read the official text ↗
Code of conduct

Voluntary Code of Conduct on the Responsible Development and Management of Advanced Generative AI Systems

Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) · Canada · 2023

Canadian code for generative AI developers: safety assessment, adversarial testing, and public transparency — a stopgap while binding federal AI legislation remains stalled.

Read the official text ↗
Treaty

Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence

Council of Europe · International · 2024

First legally binding international treaty on AI: requires parties to ensure AI systems respect human rights, democracy, and the rule of law. Binding on states once ratified; open to non-member states.
